Look up from your desk at Cobbleford Co-Working and you will see brick walls, steel trusses and windows taller than most front doors. None of that was designed for laptops. For most of the last century, 214 Millrace Road was a woolen mill, and the building still carries the marks of the work that went on inside it.

## A mill on the millrace

The mill went up in the 1920s on the bank of the river, beside the millrace - the narrow channel that once carried water to turn the wheels. That channel gave our road its name. Raw wool came in by wagon and later by truck, and left as yarn and heavy blankets sold across the region.

At its busiest, the mill employed close to a hundred people from Cobbleford and the farms around it. Ask around town and you will still meet people whose grandparents ran the looms on the second floor.

## The quiet years

Production slowed after the war, and the looms stopped for good in the 1970s. For a while the ground floor stored furniture and farm equipment, but the upper floors sat empty for decades, with pigeons for tenants and the river rolling past below.

## Bringing it back

The renovation started with one rule: keep everything that tells the building's story. The brick was cleaned rather than covered. The timber beams and steel trusses stayed exposed. The tall windows were rebuilt to match the original grid, so the light on the main floor looks much as it did a hundred years ago.

The new parts are mostly invisible. Insulation and efficient heat pumps keep the space warm through a long winter, an elevator reaches every floor, and gigabit fiber with a backup line now runs where the line shafts once spun.

## From twelve desks to three floors

Cobbleford Co-Working opened in one corner of the old mill with twelve desks. Today our members share three floors with meeting rooms, phone and podcast booths, private offices and a kitchen that looks out over the water.

## Details to look for on your visit

- Iron hooks in the ceiling beams, where drive belts once hung.
- Worn grooves in the floorboards near the windows, left by decades of rolling carts.
- The old loading dock, now the river entrance and home of our covered bike rack.
- A framed photo by the coffee bar of the mill crew lined up on the front steps.
