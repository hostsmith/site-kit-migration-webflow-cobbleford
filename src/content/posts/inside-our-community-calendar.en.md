Working remotely gives you a lot of freedom, but it can get quiet. When we ask members what made them stay, the answer is rarely the Wi-Fi or the coffee. It is the people. That is why every membership includes a full calendar of events, and why our community manager spends as much time making introductions as answering questions.

Here is what a typical month looks like.

## Thursday lunch-and-learns

Every Thursday at noon, someone takes twenty minutes in the kitchen to share what they know, followed by questions over lunch. Most talks come from members. Recent topics include:

- Bookkeeping basics for freelancers, from Sasha Moravec
- Recording clean audio in a small room, from Emerson Tull
- Running your first user interviews, from Remy Castellane
- Pricing creative work without underselling it

Bring your own lunch - we bring dessert.

![Two laptops and a coffee mug on a wooden table](/assets/images/cobbleford-laptops-coffee.jpg)

_Laptops, coffee and a long wooden table: most lunch-and-learns start like this._

## First Friday socials

On the first Friday of each month, we close the laptops at 4:30pm and gather on the main floor for snacks, cider and conversation. Members bring partners, kids and the occasional dog. It is the easiest way to meet the people you nod to at the coffee bar every morning.

## Skill shares

Skill shares are small, hands-on sessions for six to ten people in one of our meeting rooms. Think spreadsheet shortcuts, photographing products with a phone, or an hour of portfolio and resume reviews. Anyone can propose one, and they are a great way to test a workshop before you offer it to clients.

## The members-only chat

Between events, members stay in touch through our private chat. It is where people swap referrals, ask for feedback on a pitch, find out who has a spare monitor cable and organize the afternoon walk along the river.

It is also where the occasional cat video does the rounds. This one won last month's vote for the best excuse to take a break:

## How to get the most out of it

- **Pick one event a month.** You do not need to go to everything. One regular commitment is enough to start recognizing faces.
- **Say what you are working on.** The fastest way to get help, or work, is to let people know what you do.
- **Ask for an introduction.** Our community manager knows nearly everyone and is happy to connect you.
- **Offer a talk.** Twenty minutes on something you know well is the best networking there is.

As Remy Castellane, a UX researcher and member, puts it:

> “I have picked up two new clients just from chatting with other members.”
